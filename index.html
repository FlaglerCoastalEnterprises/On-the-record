About On The Record

Last updated: November 25, 2026

“Know before they vote.”

On The Record is a free interactive map of Flagler County government. Tap a building — a city hall, a courthouse, a fire station, a development site — and see what was decided there, what it cost, and what happens next. No agenda PDFs to decode and no jargon: every item is summarized in clear language and carries its source, so you can check the original yourself.

Who runs it: On The Record is published by Flagler Coastal Enterprises LLC of Palm Coast, Florida. We research the week's meetings and records, write the summaries, and keep the map current.

Editorial independence

On The Record is nonpartisan. We don't endorse candidates, campaigns or ballot measures, and we don't take positions on the decisions we cover. We summarize what local government did and link to the source so you can verify it yourself.

Trust

Sources & Methodology

Accuracy is the point of this page, so here is exactly how it is put together.

  • Official sources first. Items start from the public record: published agendas, minutes, meeting video, and staff reports from Flagler County, the cities of Palm Coast, Bunnell and Flagler Beach, the towns of Beverly Beach and Marineland, and Flagler County Schools — plus FDOT releases and official public notices.
  • Reporting is a lead, not the record. When local reporting — for example FlaglerLive — surfaces something first, we treat it as a lead and confirm it against the official record before it appears here. When an item rests on reporting, its source line names the outlet and the date.
  • Updated daily, rebuilt weekly. The week's data is reviewed and updated daily — outcomes, corrections and newly posted documents are folded in as they appear — and a full new week is loaded each week.
  • How items are chosen. An item earns a place when it involves a decision, public money, development, public safety or schools affecting Flagler County residents. Quiet buildings stay on the map and say so — a quiet week is information too.
  • Check us. Every item carries a source line, so you can go straight to the agenda, minutes, video or article it came from and read the original.

The official records we check: Flagler County CivicClerk · Flagler Schools BoardDocs · Palm Coast agenda center · City of Bunnell agendas & minutes · Flagler Beach meetings hub

Corrections

Corrections policy

If we publish something wrong, we fix it on the card, note what changed, and update the “Last updated” stamp at the top of the page. When an outcome wasn't reported yet — a vote whose minutes hadn't posted — the card says so, and is updated when the official record appears.

Spotted an error? Every building card carries a Report an error link, or email us any time at coastalenterprises386@gmail.com with the building name and what looks wrong, and we will check it against the source.

How it works · First visit

Explore Flagler County, building by building

Every public building has its own page — what happened there this week, what’s next, and when. You don’t need to read a whole agenda to follow along.

  1. 1
    Tap a pin on the map

    Gold pins had news this week. Gray pins were quiet. Tapping a pin opens that building’s card beside the map.

  2. 2
    Search or filter to find your topic

    Use the search box for a building, a board, or a topic — try “YMCA” or “animal control” — or tap a layer like Meetings, Money, or Schools. The map flies to your match.

  3. 3
    Read the card, check what’s next

    Each card gives the decision in clear language, the next meeting or deadline, and a short timeline — plus Share and Follow for that one building.
